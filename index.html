The problem

"Why did the answer change?" shouldn't take a week.

An AI pipeline is a chain of steps: retrieve, rank, build a prompt, call a model, act. Any of them can change quietly: a data refresh, a library upgrade, a new model behind the same name. When the answer moves, your team is left reading two piles of logs and hoping they line up.

Logs what your system says

  • Written by the system you're investigating, in whatever shape it chose
  • Hard to compare run against run
  • Nobody outside your team can check them

onetrace what someone else can check

  • One small receipt per step: what it read, what it produced, which tool and settings
  • Two runs compared step by step, with the first difference named
  • Checkable by anyone, offline, with an independent verifier

What you get

Answers you can trace. Changes you can explain.

Three things your team gets on day one, without changing how your pipeline works.

Find the step that changed

Compare any two runs. Every step is marked same, first difference or changed after it, so you start at the cause, not at the symptom.

Prove what ran

Each run leaves a chained record. Change, delete or reorder anything afterwards and an independent check says so. Sign it with your key if you need to.

See it all in one place

The onetrace console turns run records into pages anyone on the team can read: what was checked, what held, and what needs attention first.

Honest by design

We tell you what it doesn't prove, too.

A record you can trust has to be clear about its limits. onetrace says these out loud, in the product and in the docs.

It doesn't judge truth

A pipeline that faithfully records a wrong answer still verifies. onetrace shows what ran, not whether the answer was right.

"Same" means same output

A step marked same produced identical output. If its tool or settings changed, that's flagged separately, never hidden.

Not checked is not passed

When something couldn't be checked, onetrace says so plainly. It never reports a gap as a pass.
