Example Customer support

A rule edit re-routes a ticket. A refund window changes behind a model's varying words. Both are located.

Two support pipelines: one triages a ticket and routes it to a queue, the other drafts a reply with a model-like step whose wording changes on every run, then applies the refund policy.

Open this pipeline in the live demo All examples

Invented example data, recorded when the demo was built. The demo records and compares; it controls nothing.

The pipeline

The support reply: four steps, from a customer's question to the reply that would go out.

01 questionthe customer's question
02 drafta varying draft
03 policy_checkrefund_days
04 sendthe reply
  • question keeps the question and the ticket it came on.
  • draft writes a reply. In the demo it is a stub that picks one of a few phrasings per run, as a sampled model call varies; its receipt marks it not re-runnable.
  • policy_check sets the refund window in the reply from its recorded setting, and declines a window longer than the policy allows.
  • send records the reply that would go out.

What the demo plants

The runs, and what was changed in each.

RunWhat was changed
p4-29-reply-30-daysNothing: the baseline, with a 30-day refund window.
p4-30-reply-14-daysdraft's wording (its seed), and policy_check's refund_days, 30 to 14.
p4-31-reply-60-days-declinedA 60-day window, longer than the policy allows.
p3-23-t-1-rules1 → p3-27-t-1-rules2In the ticket-triage pipeline, the classifier's rules, version 1 to 2.

What onetrace shows

Each change, located in the record.

Past the varying draft, the setting that changed

draft's wording differs, so it is the first difference, as a model's output would be. The page still names the setting that changed after it: policy_check's refund_days, 30 to 14, and the reply that goes out now says 14 days.

A window the policy doesn't allow: declined

policy_check declines a 60-day window and records why, so the run stops there and send is left without a receipt. The run's page says so.

The rule edit in ticket triage

In the triage pipeline, version 2 of the classifier's rules checks the technical rule before the billing rule, so a ticket naming both is classed technical and routed to tech-support. The homepage lets you step through that comparison.

What it doesn't say

A located change, not a reason.

The comparison names what changed and where. It doesn't say whether a 14-day window or a rule order is right, and it doesn't judge the draft's wording. See what each verdict means and what onetrace does not claim.

Try it yourself: this pipeline in the live demo, or on your machine with pip install onetrace-console and onetrace-console demo --open.
