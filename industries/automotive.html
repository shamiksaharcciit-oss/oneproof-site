Example Automotive manufacturing

A tightening tool's torques pass a wheel or send it to rework. See what changed, and what didn't.

A tightening tool records five bolt torques for a wheel. The pipeline reads the tool's log, checks each torque against a window, and passes the wheel or sends it to rework. A firmware update that changes no reading is shown as exactly that.

Open this pipeline in the live demo All examples

Invented example data, recorded when the demo was built. The demo records and compares; it controls nothing. The screenshots are from a build of the same demo: each build gives its runs new fingerprints and times, so those differ from the live demo's, and what the screenshots show is the same.

The pipeline

Three steps, from a tool's log to a station decision.

01 read_tool_logfive torques, N·m
02 torque_check110.0 to 130.0 N·m
03 station_decisionpass or rework
  • read_tool_log reads the tightening tool's log: the five bolt torques.
  • torque_check lists each bolt whose torque is outside the window.
  • station_decision passes the wheel where no bolt is outside the window, and sends it to rework where one is.

What the demo plants

The runs, and what was changed in each.

RunWhat was changed
p9-46-torque-baselineNothing: the baseline. All five torques are inside the window.
p9-47-torque-tool-firmware-changedThe tool's firmware, 4.2 to 4.3, a setting of read_tool_log. Its readings are the same.
p9-48-torque-one-bolt-lowThe tool's log: bolt 3 reads 104.2 N·m where it read 119.6.

What onetrace shows

Each change, located in the record.

The firmware change: a setting moved, the outputs did not

The page names the setting, tool_firmware, 4.2 to 4.3. Every step's output is the same in both runs, so every step reads Same and the comparison says the two runs do not differ. A changed setting is shown even when nothing it touches moves.

One bolt low: first difference at read_tool_log, then rework

The tool's log is an input from outside the pipeline, kept as its digest, so the page shows it changed, and no setting did. read_tool_log is the first difference: bolt 3 reads 104.2 N·m. torque_check lists it outside the 110.0 to 130.0 N·m window, and station_decision says rework where it said pass.

What it doesn't say

A located change, not a reason.

The comparison shows which reading moved and the decision that followed, and that a firmware update changed no reading. It doesn't say why the bolt was under-torqued, or whether the window is right. See what each verdict means and what onetrace does not claim.

Try it yourself: this pipeline in the live demo, or on your machine with pip install onetrace-console and onetrace-console demo --open.
